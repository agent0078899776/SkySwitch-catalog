Technology Overview — Sealed Magnetic Latching Relay

Sealed magnetic latching relays are electromechanical switching devices that maintain their contact position after actuation without continuous coil power. Switching is achieved using a magnetic latching mechanism (permanent magnet or remanent magnetization), combined with a hermetically sealed contact system.

A short electrical pulse applied to the coil sets or resets the relay. Once switched, the relay remains in its state even when power is removed, significantly reducing energy consumption and heat generation.

Key Advantages

  • Zero holding power consumption (no continuous coil drive)
  • High reliability due to hermetic sealing
  • Low thermal load and improved system efficiency
  • Stable operation in vibration and shock environments

Design Features

  • Magnetic latching mechanism (single-coil or dual-coil design)
  • Hermetically sealed contact chamber (inert gas or vacuum)
  • Optimized contact materials for long electrical life
  • Compact metal package for harsh environments

Typical Limitations

  • Requires polarity control or separate SET/RESET pulses
  • More complex control circuitry compared to non-latching relays
  • Switching speed in milliseconds range

Technology Overview — Radio Frequency (RF) Relays

Radio Frequency (RF) relays are precision electromechanical switching devices designed for routing high-frequency signals while maintaining signal integrity. They are optimized for applications where low loss, high isolation, and stable impedance are critical.

RF relays operate using an electromagnetic actuator that mechanically switches signal paths. Unlike standard relays, RF designs focus on minimizing parasitic capacitance and inductance, ensuring consistent performance across a defined frequency range.

Key Advantages

  • Low insertion loss (minimal signal attenuation)
  • High isolation between signal paths
  • Stable impedance (typically 50Ω or 75Ω systems)
  • Wide frequency range (from MHz up to GHz)

Design Features

  • Controlled impedance transmission paths
  • Coaxial or stripline internal structures
  • Hermetically sealed enclosure for signal stability
  • Precision contact geometry to reduce reflection (low VSWR)

Key RF Parameters

  • Insertion Loss (dB): signal attenuation through the relay
  • Isolation (dB): separation between open signal paths
  • VSWR: impedance matching quality
  • Operating Frequency: maximum usable frequency range

Typical Limitations

  • Mechanical switching speed (slower than solid-state RF switches)
  • Limited lifetime due to contact wear
  • Performance degradation at extreme high frequencies if not properly designed

Technology Overview — Electromechanical Contactors

Electromechanical contactors are high-power switching devices designed for reliable operation in DC and AC power systems. Switching is performed by mechanically actuated contacts driven by an electromagnetic coil.

When control voltage is applied to the coil, a magnetic field is generated, pulling the armature and closing the power contacts. Upon removal of voltage, the contacts return to their original state via a spring mechanism.

Key Advantages

  • High current switching capability (tens to hundreds of amperes)
  • True galvanic isolation between control and power circuits
  • Low contact resistance in ON state
  • Robust performance in harsh environments

Design Features

  • Hermetically sealed or gas-filled contact chamber
  • Arc suppression system (magnetic blowout or gas quenching)
  • Auxiliary contacts for feedback and control logic
  • Optimized contact materials for DC load switching

Typical Limitations

  • Mechanical wear limits lifetime compared to solid-state devices
  • Slower switching speed (milliseconds range)
  • Contact arcing under high DC loads requires proper derating
